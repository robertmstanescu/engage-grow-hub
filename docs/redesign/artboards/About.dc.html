THE SUMMONING

Every haunted organisation has a vampire moment.

Robby's Magic Coffin for Stupid Vampires, est. 2010

2010. I'm in middle school, and I've just discovered True Blood, a show about vampires having a great deal of sex in the American South, which is not obviously what a middle schooler should be watching, and yet. For about eight months I am insufferable about vampires in a way only a kid that age, with that little else going on, can be.

I do what any self-respecting kid with a Facebook account does with a new obsession: I turn my parents' living room into a place. Not a place-place, a Facebook check-in. I call it Robby's Magic Coffin for Stupid Vampires, and I use it to tag my own status updates, for no reason anyone could defend, least of all me.

Years pass, and what happens next looks less like a career and more like a string of shifts. I wait tables and pull pints on weekends at Leicester Tigers and the Ricoh Arena, freeze through an English winter asking strangers on the street to fund a guide dog, then spend a year telling people over the phone that their fridge has died and a replacement is on its way. I become a bank teller in Coventry. Then Klarna: first talking distressed customers back from the edge of a decision they'll regret, then training the people who talk to distressed customers, and it's in that second job, watching someone's face change mid-session when a piece of training lands, that I work out what I've secretly wanted to do the whole time: become a part of people's adventures at work.

Also, somewhere in all of this, I collect two degrees, one in Social Anthropology and one in Media and Communication, mostly because no one could tell me no.

Then South Pole: three and a half years being the face of internal communications, DEI and HR Project Management for a 1,300-person sustainability consultancy spread across 36 countries, for a company helping bring real climate impact for all.

WHY THE COFFIN?

Some things get buried. These stay exposed.

01

Your engagement survey is a hack.

Run it, get a number, drop it in a slide, forget it by Friday. That's a seance for a culture that left the building, dressed in engagement's clothes, and the ninety days of silence after prove it. Everyone bloody clocks it.

02

HR protects the company first.

It's the fine print of the job description: implied everywhere, spelled out almost nowhere, and it outworks anything printed in bold. Once you see it, you can help the employees HR is trying to protect.

03

Culture is safety during conflict.

Culture shows up the one time someone asks an uncomfortable question in a town hall: the room stops watching the slide and starts watching leadership's face, waiting for the flinch, or they reach for garlic.

Somewhere in the middle of figuring out what came next, the coffin came back. Not the vampires, the coffin. HR calls it engagement. Comms calls it silence. Ops calls it a spreadsheet nobody updates. I call it a vampire moment: the place in an organisation where something drains away, and nobody with the right job title is looking at it.

I couldn't call the business what I called the check-in, obviously. You cannot build client relationships on the word “stupid”. So Stupid Vampires became Silly Vampires, which is really just Stupid Vampires but wearing Ralph Lauren.
